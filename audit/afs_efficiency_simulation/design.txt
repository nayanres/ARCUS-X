Synthetic AFS efficiency experiment: frozen protocol
====================================================

The primary experiment is configured by config.json. The file, simulation
source, production controller source and Python/NumPy versions are hashed
before the final run. Development and validation curves have independent seeds
and are used for adapter tests, not policy tuning. The final set has 200
parameterized curves per family, two independent noise realizations per curve,
and nine equally sized families. The pooled result depends on that mixture;
conditioning on crossing or detection changes the effective family weights.

Production controller
---------------------
simulation.py invokes BenchmarkRunner._evaluate_all_dimensions directly on a
minimal instance, replacing only _evaluate_depth_batch with synthetic batch
I/O. No search decision is reimplemented. With one tier, one gravity condition,
and no grid sweep, the runner requests one calibration observation at z=3
(gravity 0), then five responses per search batch (gravity 0.5). The anchor
is a different probe identity, ignored by the search and charged to all
policies. The initial search bounds are 1 and 40, first midpoint 20, and
integer midpoints use floor division. A valid batch mean below 0.5 moves the
upper bound down and records a candidate; a passing or all-invalid batch moves
the lower bound up. If the midpoint reaches the upper bound while passing,
the bound doubles up to 150. The loop terminates when the bounds cross or its
iteration guard fires. With no candidate and a lower bound beyond 150, the
native output is 150; 0 is also possible when no candidate was established.
The controller does not perform the separate post-hoc recovery look-ahead.
This search can expand even after a candidate was recorded. An adapter test
asserts the production trace [20,30,25,27,28,29,44,36,32] for a deterministic
scenario, including one all-invalid batch.

The adapter supplies per-response valid flags and fractions of correct
post-initial positions over all z supplied actions, matching live Step Accuracy.
Batch means use BenchmarkRunner._is_cognitive_valid. Invalid probes consume
budget and do not enter the mean. Identity-based reuse of a completed batch is
free; calibration and search observations have distinct identities. This
experiment does not simulate provider-specific error categories or model text.

Environment and estimators
--------------------------
For each curve, the equations and parameter ranges are in config.json and
environment(). Exponential and logistic curves are bounded by their low/high
parameters; abrupt and recovery curves are piecewise constant; gradual is
linear; oscillatory and near-threshold curves use sinusoidal components;
censored curves either stay above 0.5 or cross close to 150. Validity is one
except in the validity-degradation family, which uses a decreasing logistic
and optionally a zero-validity cutoff. Values are clipped to [0.001,0.999].

Conditional on validity, each probe draws q ~ Beta(30p,30(1-p)); then the
number of correct transition positions is Binomial(z,q). The score is that
count divided by z. This exchangeable beta-binomial model induces intraclass
correlation 1/31 across positions within a probe; it does not model coherent
state trajectories or error persistence. A fixed potential observation is
keyed by split, family, curve, replicate, horizon and probe index through a
deterministic horizon-level random stream. It is common to all policies and
independent of request order.

All policies get the same ignored, charged calibration response. Their
predefined schedules and alternative adaptive rule are in config.json.
Policies do not access the generating curve; only the evaluator sees it.
The fixed-grid policy samples the 16 prespecified points 1, 11, ..., 141,
150 and then stops, so it cannot use more than 81 responses even when the
available budget is 161. This is a fixed ten-step grid, not a budget-specific
grid optimized separately for each budget. Other policies can keep sampling
until their own stopping rule or the budget is reached.
The common first-crossing estimator is the smallest observed horizon with a
valid mean below 0.5. No observed crossing is reported as undetected; for
true no-crossing curves, the corresponding correct-censor rate is the
fraction with no observed crossing, not evidence that the policy proved
absence of a crossing. First and persistent absolute errors are reported only
when both truth and estimate exist, with detection and failure separately.
Persistent estimates require five consecutive sampled integer horizons with
valid means below threshold; the policy-neutral truth requires five
consecutive integer horizons with generating p below threshold. Threshold
region coverage is the fraction of true |p-0.5|<=0.05 horizons sampled;
region precision measures how many observed means in the band are truly in it.
Cost-to-target is the first prefix reaching +/-3 or +/-5 under the common
estimator, and is conditional on success; report the ever-achieved fraction
too. Final within-tolerance rates use the estimate at the evaluated budget,
which can differ from an earlier transient success.

Budget grid 11,21,41,81,161 is fixed from the controller configuration:
one calibration plus 2,4,8,16,32 complete five-probe batches. An early
terminated policy does not spend the remaining budget. A separate comparison
evaluates each baseline at the actual total cost of the AFS run. Curves, not
repeated realizations or budgets, are the bootstrap unit. Percentile intervals
use 500 curve-cluster draws; paired policy differences retain only outcomes
available for both policies on the same realization.
The separate operational comparison uses the production native candidate
only once AFS terminates; native zero, ceiling saturation without a below-floor
batch, and budget interruption are not treated as candidate estimates.
Baselines use their earliest observed below-floor horizon as a comparator.

Sensitivity
-----------
The primary AFS threshold remains 0.5. Separately labeled 0.4 and 0.6
controller runs and 3- and 10-horizon persistence targets assess threshold
and persistence sensitivity. The separately specified noise_stress.py uses
the first 50 final curves in each family, a separate observation stream, beta
concentrations 5, 30 and 200 (intraclass correlations 1/6, 1/31, 1/201),
and response budgets 41 and 81. This is a sensitivity check, not tuning.
Stress summaries also retain per-curve parameters and valid-response counts.
Invalidity, slope, location, non-monotonicity and budgets can be examined by
family and factors. The complete finite synthetic domain is known only to the
evaluator; no unobserved model outcomes are invented.

Run:
  python -m pytest audit\afs_efficiency_simulation\test_simulation.py -q
  python audit\afs_efficiency_simulation\simulation.py --split test
  python audit\afs_efficiency_simulation\noise_stress.py
  python audit\afs_efficiency_simulation\analyze.py

Family checkpoints record file hashes and reject mismatched source or
environment metadata; rerun the same simulation command to resume.
